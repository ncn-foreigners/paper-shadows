\begin{abstract}

Estimating the size of hidden populations typically requires linking individual records across multiple sources, such as by capture–recapture methods, which is often either impossible or error-prone. We estimate unauthorized foreign populations from aggregated administrative data alone, by proposing a~general community-anchored modeling framework. Our model represents the unobserved unauthorized stock as a power-law function of an observed authorized reference population with the exponent as the community-anchor parameter, while the detection of unauthorized foreigners is regulated by an exposure-elasticity parameter, where both the parameters vary with covariates. Applied to Poland over 2019–2024, the method yields an estimated adult non-Schengen unauthorized population of 94,000 (80\% interval: 36,000–206,000) in 2019 and 101,000 (46,000–177,000) in 2024, compared to the authorized reference population that has doubled over this period from 0.6 to 1.2 million. Moreover, we recover a~structural break: the Ukrainian component declined from 60,000 to 11,000 following the 2022 EU Temporary Protection Directive, while the non-Ukrainian population expanded from 34,000 to 89,000. By removing the need for record linkage, our approach offers a~viable alternative for many countries.

\textbf{Keywords}: Hidden populations, Aggregate data, Community-anchored models, Unauthorized migration, Official Statistics

\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

In 2025, YouGov conducted a~survey in France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Denmark, Poland, and the UK, finding that support for reducing
immigration is high and that these sentiments may be influenced by misperceptions about the scale of irregular migration. For example, in
Poland, 36\% believed that there are more unauthorized than lawful migrants, compared to 28\% who think the opposite, and 22\% who believe the
numbers are roughly equal \citep{yougov2025immigration}. At the same time, close to 5 million migrants were registered in the Polish
population register (PESEL) at the end of 2025, while those detected by Polish authorities as staying illegally numbered only about 10,000,
though this figure captures only detected cases, not the total unauthorized population \citep{border-guards-2025}. \cite{connor2019europe} estimated the unauthorized population of Poland at below 100,000 in 2014, 2015 and 2017 and at 100,000--200,000 in 2016.

This type of mistaken belief is not new and is driven by various factors \citep[cf.][]{alesina2023immigration, ajzenman2023immigration, lutz2023misperceptions}. Given that misperceptions stem from being misinformed and uninformed, plausible estimates of the hidden population size are essential for designing public policies, allocating public funding, and planning public services. 

The size of the native-born population in a~given country may be reasonably well-known at the national level, although the methods vary greatly across countries due to differences in statistical infrastructure: presence or absence of population registers (as in the UK and USA), variation in register quality, reliance on large-scale sample surveys (such as the American Community Survey; ACS) or population censuses. See e.g. \citet{skinner2018issues} or \citet{dunne2024system}.

Foreign nationals can be generally divided into two main groups: those who stay or reside legally with valid permits, and those who are unauthorized---staying or residing without valid documentation. The latter group, in particular, is widely recognized as hard-to-reach and hard-to-measure
\citep{mckenzie2009surveying,mendez2013surveying}, including the countries that have high-quality population registers \citep{danko2024assessing,monti2020over, careja2018using}, as by definition these people are excluded from the population register or other important registers, and also have incentives to avoid detection. In this paper, we focus on the unauthorized population.

Poland provides a~case-in-point that illustrates the estimation challenge. First, between 2015 and 2024, the registered foreign nationals increased from 200,000 to nearly 4.5 million\footnote{These figures are based on the population (PESEL hereinafter) register and indicate the number of people who registered for a~PESEL identifier, not how many actually reside in Poland. We discuss other registers later in the paper.}. Second, between 2018 and 2023, Poland issued over 6 million visas\footnote{This number refers to all visas types; it includes short-term visas, renewals, and all consular visa issuances. For more details see \citet{nik2024fullreport_en}.} (about 3.8 million of which were work visas), placing it among the EU countries issuing the most visas of this type. Third, following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, over 3.5 million refugees transited through Poland between February and mid-May 2022, with about 1 million remaining under temporary protection as of 31 December 2025 (cf. \cite{stats-poland-2024, duszczyk2022war, duszczyk2023war}, and \url{https://ukraincywpolsce.stat.gov.pl}).
Finally, from 2021, Russia and Belarus have placed pressure on the Belarusian-Polish-EU border through politically instrumentalized migration, influencing the scale of unauthorized entry, but with limited political implications \citep{Charasz2025}. This combination of rapid demographic change, high visa issuance, humanitarian flows, and border pressures creates conditions where unauthorized populations are likely substantial but hard to estimate.

In this paper, we propose a~general modeling framework for estimating the size of the unauthorized foreign population, building on the community-anchored model introduced by \citet{Zhang2008} for a~one-off application to Norwegian data. \citet{Zhang2008} established identifiability of the power-law relationship between authorized and unauthorized populations under Poisson log-linearity; we extend it into a~framework that accommodates data over multiple years and subpopulations, with covariate-varying parameters, robust inference of uncertainty, and estimator choice guided by simulation evidence. 

Unlike other existing approaches --- such as the residual method 
\citep[cf.][]{warren1987count, chassamboulli2015labor, van2021uncertainty, Warren2025},
capture-recapture or multiple systems estimation
\citep[cf.][]{chipperfield2024robust, bohning2024one, aleshin2024central},
and deductive or digital-trace methods \citep{recchi2026overstayers} --- which require linkage across multiple data sources or unit-level micro data at specific time points, our method requires only aggregated data. We develop a~model grounded in demographic and behavioral assumptions that uses three inputs: \emph{aggregated counts} of detected unauthorized migrants, the size of the authorized foreign nationals, and a~covariate proxying for detection intensity. 

Our key assumptions include: (1) there exists a~structural relationship between unauthorized and authorized foreign nationals due to the former's reliance on communities or networks for survival; (2) it is valid to target a~theoretical size of unauthorized population without spurious day-to-day variations; and (3) the detection rate of unauthorized foreigners can be modeled statistically as a~function of known features that proxy the relevant channels or mechanisms.

Support of such a~theoretical model can be found in two earlier EU-funded research programs. The CLANDESTINO project
\citep{CLANDESTINO2009} documented the extent to which unauthorized migrants depend on established co-ethnic communities for housing,
employment, and social support --- the same network mechanism is captured by the community anchor parameter in our model. The MIrreM project \citep{kierans2025handbook} highlighted severe biases of sizing by raw apprehension data directly, which reflect enforcement priorities and capacity constraints rather than the underlying unauthorized population --- the insight is reflected in our detection intensity parameter which connects to the observed counts of unauthorized people to the unobserved population sizes. Our modeling framework thus answers a~call form both these projects for methods that move beyond the contested administrative counts toward credible population estimates.
 
Applied to the Polish data from 2019 to 2024, our approach yields plausible trajectory of estimates despite the structural shock caused by the Ukraine war. Our estimate of the adult (18+) non-Schengen unauthorized population size in Poland stands at about 94,000 (80\% interval: 36,000--206,000) in 2019 and 101,000 (80\% interval: 46,000--177,000) in 2024.\footnote{In the abstract and introduction we report 80\% intervals; the tables report both 80\% and 95\% intervals. Because the bootstrap distribution is right-skewed, the interval bounds indicate estimation uncertainty rather than equally plausible population sizes, and the central (median) estimate is the most probable value. Point estimates and interval bounds for unauthorized population sizes are rounded to the nearest 1,000; detailed tables retain finer precision.} A central message is one of relative decline: even as Poland's registered foreign population surged and the Social Insurance Institution (ZUS) reference population almost doubled (from 612,000 to 1,134,000), the estimated unauthorized population remained broadly stable, so its share of the ZUS reference population fell from 15.4\% in 2019 to 8.9\% in 2024. This relative finding is robust to the considerable width of the underlying confidence intervals.

Moreover, our estimates capture divergent dynamics by the country of origin. The estimated Ukrainian unauthorized population declines from 60,000 in 2019 to 11,000 in 2024, consistent with the mass regularization under the 2022 EU Temporary Protection Directive, while the non-Ukrainian component moves in the opposite direction, rising from 34,000 to 89,000 over the same period. 

Sensitivity analyses suggest that these estimates are robust against model mis-specifications that are unavoidable, and aligns plausibly with other administrative data used in the estimation. In particular, the wide confidence intervals reflect the inherent uncertainty in estimating hidden populations \citep[cf.~][]{van2021uncertainty}, and it is important for users and policy makers to pay attention to the range of plausible values rather than relying too much on any single point estimate.

Finally, our approach is easily transferable to other countries, since it relies only on aggregated administrative data that exist commonly, and its efficacy has already been demonstrated in quite different situations from Norway to Poland. The relevant methodology has the potential for National Statistical Offices to improve migration estimates by adding the \emph{uncounted} component.

In the rest of the paper, Section~\ref{sec-theory} presents the theoretical model, Section~\ref{sec-empirical} the empirical specification, 
Sections~\ref{sec-data}--\ref{sec-results} describe the application to Polish data, and the conclusions are drawn in Section~\ref{sec-conclusions}. Additional details are given in the Supplementary Materials. The open-source R \citep{r-cran} package \texttt{uncounted} is available at \url{https://github.com/ncn-foreigners/uncounted}. Replication materials are available here \url{https://github.com/ncn-foreigners/paper-shadows}.


\section{Theoretical model}\label{sec-theory}

To formalize our theoretical model, let $M_{it}$ be the size of the unauthorized population of \emph{community}\footnote{Note that we use the term community to indicate that our approach can also be applied where data at the country-of-origin level are not available (e.g. countries are grouped by ethnicity).} $i$ $(i=1,\ldots,I)$ in period $t$ $(t=1,\ldots,T)$, and let $N_{it}$ denote the size of a~corresponding authorized \emph{reference population}. Let $m_{it}$ be the \emph{detected} count of unauthorized migrants (among the $M_{it}$), and let $n_{it}$ denote a~known count which may or may not correspond to a~subset among the $N_{it}$, such that $n_{it}/N_{it}$ provides a~feature for \emph{detection-intensity} related to $m_{it}/M_{it}$.

We define the \emph{theoretical} size of unauthorized population as the conditional expectation of $M_{it}$‚
\begin{equation}
\xi_{it} = \mathbb{E}(M_{it} \mid N_{it}).
\label{eq-xi-expectation}
\end{equation} 
Let the conditional expectation of the \emph{detection rate} $p_{it} = m_{it}/M_{it}$ given the others be 
\begin{equation}
\rho_{it} = \mathbb{E}(p_{it} \mid M_{it}, N_{it}, n_{it}).
\label{eq-rho-expectation}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq-xi-expectation} and \eqref{eq-rho-expectation} under the assumptions (i) $M_{it}$ and $p_{it}$ are conditionally independent given $(N_{it}, n_{it})$, and (ii) $n_{it}$ provides no additional information about $M_{it}$ beyond $N_{it}$, we obtain the expectation of the detected count $m_{it} = M_{it} p_{it}$ as 
\begin{equation}
\mu_{it} = \mathbb{E}\{ M_{it}\cdot \mathbb{E}(p_{it} \mid M_{it}) \mid N_{it}, n_{it}\} 
= \mathbb{E}(M_{it} \mid N_{it}) \cdot \mathbb{E}(p_{it} \mid N_{it},n_{it}) = \xi_{it} \cdot \rho_{it}
\label{eq-counts-expectation}
\end{equation}
The functional forms of $\xi_{it}$ and $\rho_{it}$, as well as the distributional assumptions of $m_{it}$, will be treated later as part of our empirical model in Section \ref{sec-empirical}. Below we discuss first the motivation and justification of the theoretical model defined by \eqref{eq-xi-expectation}--\eqref{eq-counts-expectation}.

First, our core assumption is that the size of the unauthorized population is structurally related to the authorized population via communities, which typically concur with the country of origin, ethnicity, or similar characteristics. This is supported by migration research showing that unauthorized migrants depend heavily on co-ethnic and kin-based networks for survival and incorporation into host societies
\citep{Hagan1998, Engbersen2001, Ambrosini2023}. Such networks serve multiple functions: providing accommodation, information, informal
employment, and emotional support, and often substitute for formal welfare and labour market institutions that unauthorized migrants cannot
access \citep{Anderson2007, McDowell2009}. Consequently, we expect the unauthorized population from a~given origin country to scale with the
corresponding authorized population, which serves as the foundation for these support networks.

Moreover, unlike the residual or capture-recapture methods, which treat the unauthorized population size as an \emph{unknown} constant, we view $M_{it}$ as a~\emph{random} variable arsing from processes driven by economic, political, and demographic factors, and model its theoretical size $\xi_{it}$ in relation to a~suitable reference population size $N_{it}$, i.e. Equation \eqref{eq-xi-expectation}. This allows us to capture how the two populations co-evolve: as authorized communities grow, they provide expanded networks that support larger unauthorized populations from the same origin or ethnicity. Conditioning on $N_{it}$ also makes it possible to interpret the estimate of $\xi_{it}$ without spurious day-to-day variations, just like how standard official population statistics are received. 

Regarding the detection rate $p_{it}$ that connects the observed count $m_{it}$ and the unobserved population size $M_{it}$, the factorization in \eqref{eq-counts-expectation} requires that $\mathbb{E}(p_{it} \mid M_{it}, N_{it}, n_{it}) = \mathbb{E}(p_{it} \mid N_{it}, n_{it})$,
meaning the detection rate does not depend on the size of the unauthorized population. This assumption rules out feedback
effects whereby detection intensity $\rho_{it}$ responds endogenously to $M_{it}$. Although it cannot be tested directly since $M_{it}$ is unobserved, the data of staffing and enforcement activities in Poland (see Table~\ref{tbl-appen-border-guard} in Supplementary Materials) show that legality-of-stay inspections fluctuated over 2019--2024 without a~systematic trend, Police and Border Guard staffing remained broadly stable despite substantial growth of the presence of foreign nationals, whereas employment legality inspections actually declined over the period. Altogether they suggest that detection intensity is shaped by supply-side factors (budget cycles, staffing constraints, pandemic disruptions) rather than by endogenous responses to $M_{it}$, rendering the no-feedback assumption empirically plausible.

Of course, we must caution against taking this for granted. For example, congestion effects may arise when enforcement resources are fixed relative to the unauthorized population, causing the detection rate to decline as $M_{it}$ increases \citep{fender1999general, hanson1999illegal}. Conversely, concentration effects may cause detection rate to increase with $M_{it}$ if larger populations are more visible to enforcement \citep{ethier1986illegal}. However, without additional data or instrumental variables, it is infeasible to distinguish the variation in $p_{it}$ from that in $M_{it}$ when the former depends on the latter \citep{link2003nonidentifiability}. In other words, different approaches will be necessary in such situations. 

Finally, by way of introduction to the empirical model, we notice the flexibility of this theoretical model. On the one hand, it allows one to estimate the total size of unauthorized population without decomposing the counts $m_{it}$ into visa overstayers, border apprehensions, and so on. Attempts to deal with these subgroups separately, originated from different data sources, may cause complications and aggregation inconsistencies \citep{warren1987count}. On the other hand, it is possible to disaggregate $m_{it}$ by relevant covariates such as gender and age categories, when it comes to the empirical model specification, provided this is sensible and supported by the available data. 



\section{Empirical model}\label{sec-empirical}

\subsection{Parametric specification}\label{parametric-specification}

As the first part of our empirical model, we now specify the functional forms for \eqref{eq-xi-expectation} and \eqref{eq-rho-expectation}. For the theoretical size of unauthorized population, a~power-law relationship is given as
\begin{equation}\label{eq-xi-form}
\xi_{it} = \mathbb{E}(M_{it} \mid N_{it}) = N_{it}^{\alpha_{it}},
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{it} > 0$ is an unknown \emph{Community Anchor} parameter, capturing how the unauthorized population scales with the reference population for the given community and time. Let the corresponding detection intensity $\rho_{it}$ be given as
\begin{equation}\label{eq-rho-form}
\rho_{it} = \mathbb{E}(p_{it} \mid N_{it}, n_{it}) = \left( \gamma + \frac{n_{it}}{N_{it}}\right)^{\beta_{it}},
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{it} >0$ is an unknown parameter of \emph{Exposure Elasticity}, regulating how the detection intensity responds to the chosen feature $n_{it} / N_{it}$, and $\gamma > 0$ represents a~\emph{(baseline) offset} even when $n_{it} = 0$. Notice that $\rho_{it} \in (0,1)$ as long as $0< \gamma + n_{it} / N_{it} <1$.

The community-anchored model of \citet{Zhang2008} is a~special case that uses the simplest parameterization $(\alpha_{it}, \beta_{it}, \gamma) \equiv (\alpha, \beta, 0)$ for a~one-off application in Norway, where $t$ refers to a~single year 2006 and $i$ refers to the countries of citizenship. In particular, all the countries with $m_i n_i =0$ are \emph{regrouped} into a~`rest' community, so that $\rho_{it} >0$ everywhere even with $\gamma \equiv 0$. The parameterization here in terms of \eqref{eq-xi-form} and \eqref{eq-rho-form} is more general and principled.

Firstly, to accommodate multiple periods and flexible formulation of communities, we allow $\alpha_{it}$ to vary with $(x_i, t)$, where $x_i$ denotes the relevant covariates of the communities. For example, let $x_i =1$ for males and $x_i = 2$ for females from country $i$. By suitable dummy-coding of $(x_i, t)$, we can allow distinct $\alpha$-values from crossing $x_i$ and $t$ directly, or as the sum of a~gender effect and a~year effect but without any interaction of the two. Similarly for $\beta_{it}$, with a~separate parameterization, such as $\beta(t)$ and $\alpha(x_i, t)$ at the same time. 

Next, conceptually speaking, the detection intensity $\rho_{it}$ is never zero since, even when the feature count is $n_{it} = 0$, it is still possible to detect unauthorized migrants through various channels, such as border guards, hospital admissions, rejected visa or asylum applications, employer
inspections. Introducing the offset $\gamma$ is a~more principled approach than regrouping to form ad hoc `rest' communities, because the composition of such `rest' communities will not be the same over time. Moreover, it allows one to make better use of the data points with $m_{it} >0$ and $n_{it} =0$.


\subsection{Motivation and justification of the parametric specification}\label{sec-emp-just}

Various functional forms could relate $\xi_{it}$ to $N_{it}$. Our choice of the power-law specification \eqref{eq-xi-form} is consistent with the 
considerations underlying the theoretical assumption \eqref{eq-xi-expectation}. Unauthorized migrants lack access to regular employment and welfare services, requiring a~network of contacts to survive \citep{Hagan1998, Engbersen2001}. These contacts most likely include usual residents in established socio-economic situations, which provides the ``existence basis'' for the unauthorized population, analogous to how a~reproductive
population supports offspring in demographic models.

It is therefore natural to expect a~relationship where $\xi_{it}$ increases with $N_{it}$, but the rate of increase declines as $N_{it}$ becomes
larger\footnote{\citet{massey1990social} introduced the idea of
 cumulative causation in migration, emphasizing how migrant networks
 create self-reinforcing migration processes. \citet{fussell2004limits}
 show that these mechanisms are much weaker in large urban settings
 than in smaller communities. \citet{carrington1996migration} provide a
 model in which moving costs decline as migrant networks expand,
 generating gradual self-reinforcing migration dynamics.}. In particular, our
specification closely relates to the \cite{beverton_holt_1957} model, which is a~discrete-time population model given by
\[
R = \frac{\nu S}{1 + \kappa S},
\]
where $S$ is the current population size, $R$ is the next-generation population, $\kappa$ is the density dependence parameter, and $\nu/\kappa$ is the carrying capacity. The power curve $N^{\alpha}$ and the Beverton-Holt curve exhibit similar density-dependent behavior, particularly over empirically relevant ranges of $N$. The key difference is that the Beverton-Holt model assumes an asymptotic ceiling $\nu/\kappa$, whereas the power-law specification allows $\xi_{it} \to \infty$ as $N_{it} \to \infty$. An absolute ceiling of the unauthorized population size is not necessary in our context.

In contrast, the power-law specification \eqref{eq-rho-form} does not suggest a~direct connection from the chosen feature rate $n_{it}/N_{it}$ to the detection rate $m_{it}/M_{it}$ of unauthorized migrants. The count $n_{it}$ should be chosen such that the corresponding rate $n_{it}/N_{it}$ may proxy for detection intensity through various mechanisms, the elasticity of which can be tuned by the parameter $\beta_{it}$. For instance, if $n_{it}$ is based on police records of foreign nationals facing criminal charges, there is no direct connection between criminal behavior and unauthorized status---rather, contact with law enforcement creates opportunities for detection regardless of criminality. Similarly, when $n_{it}$ is derived from events recorded in administrative systems, it may proxy the degree to which migrants interact with the relevant institutions or agencies that may detect unauthorized foreigners (e.g., hospital admissions, rejections of visa/asylum applications from the previous year).

We notice that it is possible to adopt logistic functions to ensure $\alpha_{it} <1$ or $\rho_{it} <1$; see Supplementary Materials Section~\ref{sec-appendix-ommited-proofs}. However, log transformation corresponding to the power-specification is preferable because it enables us to combine equations \eqref{eq-xi-form} and \eqref{eq-rho-form} directly on the log scale, as long as the restrictions are empirically unnecessary for the data at hand.

We obtain straightforward model diagnostics as one of the benefits. Plugging in $m_{it}$ for $\mu_{it} = \xi_{it} \rho_{it}$ given by \eqref{eq-xi-form} and \eqref{eq-rho-form}, dividing both sides by $N_{it}$ and taking logarithms for the data points with $n_{it} > 0$ yields, for each period $t$ with $(\alpha_{it}, \beta_{it}, \gamma) = (\alpha_t, \beta_t, 0)$, 
\begin{equation}\label{eq-diagnostic-model}
\log\left(\frac{m_{it}}{N_{it}}\right) = (\alpha_t - 1) \log N_{it} + \beta_t \log\left(\frac{n_{it}}{N_{it}}\right).
\end{equation}
Plotting $\log(m_{it}/N_{it})$ against each covariate on the right side provides a~diagnostic check: by the considerations above, we expect $\alpha_t - 1 < 0$, i.e., a~negative relationship with $\log N_{it}$, and $\beta_t > 0$, i.e., a~positive relationship with $\log(n_{it}/N_{it})$. Furthermore, stratifying these plots by country groups (e.g., by visa requirements, continent or migration corridor) allows us to assess whether a~single pattern holds uniformly or systematic differences exist to warrant more disaggregated parameterization. Similarly with other partitions of all the data points for exploratory data analysis. 

Finally, although the model can be tested empirically for goodness-of-fit to observed counts $m_{it}$, the specifications \eqref{eq-xi-form} and \eqref{eq-rho-form} cannot be separately verified. In other words, it is logically possible for other empirical models of $\mu_{it}$ to fit equally well to the observed counts $m_{it}$, such that a~choice must rely on certain considerations that cannot be directly tested. This is a~fundamental limitation inherent to estimating hidden populations, and we would caution against overconfidence in the results. Nevertheless, we have not found decisive evidence against specifications \eqref{eq-xi-form} and \eqref{eq-rho-form} in our applications, and the motivations outlined above provide a~coherent rationale.


\subsection{Parameter estimation}\label{estimation}
 
To estimate the mean structure \eqref{eq-xi-form}--\eqref{eq-rho-form}, it suffices to assume independent Poisson distributions of $m_{it}$, denoted by 
\begin{equation}\label{eq-poisson-model}
m_{it} \sim \text{Poisson}(\mu_{it}), \quad \log(\mu_{it}) = \alpha_{it}(\eta) \log N_{it} + \beta_{it}(\eta) \log\left(\gamma + \frac{n_{it}}{N_{it}}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\eta$ contains all the unknown constants in the specifications of $(\alpha_{it}, \beta_{it})$, and $\Pr(m_{it}=0) >0$ exists by the Poisson distribution such that all the data points can be used in the estimation directly. However, since the variance structure of $m_{it}$ is most likely misspecified in this way, we shall refer to the resulting estimator as a~pseudo maximum likelihood estimator (PMLE), instead of MLE given the Poisson distribution. The Poisson PMLE would coincide with the MLE when the Poisson distribution holds, but it requires only correct mean specification for consistency regardless of the true variance structure \citep{SantosSilvaTenreyro2006}. 

Notice that \citet{Zhang2008} accommodates over-dispersion of $m_{it}$ by explicitly assuming 
\[
m_{it} \sim \text{Poisson}(\mu_{it} u_{it}) \quad\text{and}\quad u_{it} \sim \text{Gamma}(1, \phi),
\]
where $\text{Gamma}(1, \phi)$ denotes the gamma distribution with expectation 1 and variance $1/\phi$. The Poisson-Gamma mixture yields the Negative Binomial (NB) likelihood on integrating out the unobserved random effects $u_{it}$, and the corresponding NB-MLE. In Norway applications, the NB-MLE was obtained with $\gamma \equiv 0$ in \eqref{eq-rho-form} and regrouping of data points to avoid $m_i n_i =0$. 

Given non-trivial offset $\gamma >0$, the NB-MLE is feasible without regrouping data points. Due to its ability to accommodate over-dispersion of $m_{it}$, which exists commonly in real data, the formal model-selection criteria such as AIC or BIC tend to favor NB-MLE to Poisson PMLE. However, as we explain in Supplementary Materials \ref{sec-append-why-poisson} that there are several reasons for preferring the Poisson PMLE to NB-MLE in practice, specially when non-trivial offset $\gamma >0$ is allowed in \eqref{eq-rho-form}. 


 
\subsection{Estimation of unauthorized population size}\label{estimating-the-unauthorized-population-size-and-its-variance}

Given the estimates $\hat{\alpha}_{it} = \alpha_{it}(\hat{\eta})$ from \eqref{eq-poisson-model}, we can estimate the overall size of unauthorized population for a~period $t$ as
\begin{equation}\label{eq-estimators}
\hat{\xi}_{t} = \sum_{i=1}^{I} N_{it}^{\hat{\alpha}_{it}} 
\end{equation}
Consistency of $\hat{\xi}_t$ follows from the Continuous Mapping Theorem applied to $g(\hat{\eta})$. Meanwhile, in finite-sample applications, one may consider to reduce the potential bias due to the non-linear transformation involved, which would result in an estimator given as 
\begin{equation}\label{eq-bias-correction}
\hat{\xi}_t^{\text{BC}} = \sum_{i=1}^{I} N_{it}^{\hat{\alpha}_{it}} \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (\log N_{it})^2 \sigma_{it}^2 \right),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{it}^2$ is a~term related to the variance of $\hat{\eta}$ --- see Supplementary Materials Section~\ref{sec-appendix-ommited-proofs} for the full derivation and Supplementary Materials Section~\ref{sec-appendix-sim-study} for finite-sample simulation results. However, the adjustment is typically small compared to the width of confidence interval. 

A confidence interval of $\alpha_{it}$, denoted by $(\hat{\alpha}_{it}^{\text{lower}}, \hat{\alpha}_{it}^{\text{upper}})$, can be obtained using heteroskedasticity-consistent (HC) standard errors \citep{mackinnon1985some}. Since $g(\alpha) = \sum_{i=1}^{I} N_{it}^{\alpha}$ is monotonically increasing in $\alpha$ given $N_{it} \geq 1$, a~confidence level of $\xi_t$ is given directly as
\begin{equation}\label{eq-xi-confint}
\left( \sum_{i=1}^{I} N_{it}^{\hat{\alpha}_{it}^{\text{lower}}}, \;
\sum_{i=1}^{I} N_{it}^{\hat{\alpha}_{it}^{\text{upper}}} \right).
\end{equation}
However, in practice, one may still encounter situations where the interval can be excessively wide due to ill-behaving $(\hat{\alpha}_{it}^{\text{lower}}, \hat{\alpha}_{it}^{\text{upper}})$ for a~subset of all $\alpha_{it}$.

We therefore prefer the fractional weighted bootstrap {[}FWB; \citet{XuEtAl2020}{]} with clustering by country-of-origin.
The approach simultaneously accounts for within-country correlation across years and covariates, the nonlinear transformation of parameter estimates, and their skewed distributions. In particular, one may consider the bootstrap median of $\{\hat{\xi}_t\}$ as the point estimate, which is more robust to skewness than either a~plug-in estimator or the bootstrap mean.

It is worth noting that, in applications, wide confidence intervals are a~well-documented feature of unauthorized population estimation, reflecting the inherent uncertainty in estimating hidden populations. This has been discussed extensively in the migration literature, including \citet{van2021uncertainty}, the Centre on
Migration, Policy and Society (COMPAS) estimates at the University of Oxford\footnote{See
 \url{https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/resources/briefings/unauthorized-migration-in-the-uk/}}, the CLANDESTINO project \citep[cf.][]{CLANDESTINO2009}, and the MIrreM project \citep{kierans2025handbook}.

\section{Data sources} \label{sec-data}


\subsection{Definition of an unauthorized
stay}\label{definition-of-an-unauthorized-stay}

In our study, we use Polish Border Guard reports, which distinguish
three groups of migrants: (G1) refusals of entry at the border, (G2)
detections during irregular border crossings, and (G3) detections for
illegal stay within the country or at the border when leaving. We focus
on the third group, and in particular those who were identified
\emph{within the country}. Groups G1 and G2 capture border-level
encounters (attempted or actual irregular crossings), whereas group G3
reflects detections of unauthorized migrants already present on Polish
territory, which corresponds to the target population $M_{it}$ in our
theoretical model. Within group G3, we exclude those detected at the
border upon departure, as they are in the process of leaving the
unauthorized population and their inclusion could conflate exit flows
with the resident stock. Detailed definitions of each group are provided
in Supplementary Materials Section~\ref{sec-appen-definitions}.

Under Polish law, an unauthorized stay encompasses any presence that
violates entry or residence regulations---ranging from expired visas and
overstayed permits to illegal employment and Schengen Information System 
flagged individuals.
Two authorities verify legality of stay: the Border Guard (at the border
and within the country) and the Police. Note that a~person who crossed
the border illegally but was subsequently detected within the country
falls into our analysis sample under group G3. For the full legal
definition, consequences, and recent legislative changes, see
Supplementary Materials Section~\ref{sec-appen-definitions} and~\ref{sec-appen-changes-law}. For a~comprehensive overview of the
forms and drivers of migrant irregularity in Poland---including visa
overstaying, fictitious employment declarations, and expired
pandemic-era permits---see \citet{yeliseyeu2024poland}. Finally, The Police record foreigners mainly in pre-trial criminal proceedings, which cover offences prosecuted
\emph{ex officio} and not unauthorized stay, a~petty offence handled by
the Border Guard; the two sources therefore overlap only marginally (see Section \ref{sec-appen-police}).


\subsection{Data sources}\label{data-sources}

\subsubsection{Observed unauthorized
population}\label{observed-unauthorized-population}

The first dataset, on the number of unauthorized migrants detected for
illegal stay, was prepared by representatives of the Polish Border
Guards on a~half-year basis with information on people who were
identified multiple times, along with their country of citizenship, sex
and age group. In our analysis we use counts of unique individuals.

Figure~\ref{fig-within-country} presents the number of observed
unauthorized migrants by place of apprehension by the Polish Border
Guard for the period 2014--2024, aggregated by half-year periods. The
observed pattern is dominated by apprehensions at the Polish-Ukrainian
border, which rose sharply from around 1,500 in early 2014 to a~peak
exceeding 10,000 in the first half of 2018, before declining after 2020
to levels comparable to those at other borders. This surge likely
reflects the aftermath of the 2014 conflict in eastern Ukraine and
subsequent economic migration pressures. Interior apprehensions
(``Interior'') show a~steadier upward trend, becoming the dominant
category by 2023--2024, suggesting a~shift in migration patterns or
enforcement priorities toward identifying unauthorized migrants already
within Poland rather than at exit.

The choice of reference population counts $N$ affects primarily the community-anchor parameter $\alpha$ and secondarily the exposure-elasticity parameter $\beta$. We decided to use the Social Insurance Institution (ZUS) register as the source of reference population counts $N$ for several reasons. First,
this register is updated monthly, providing timely information on the
foreign nationals. Second, it contains records of foreign individuals
registered for social insurance by their employers, which provides a~strong signal of actual presence; since registration implies active
employment, we can be reasonably certain that these foreigners are
indeed residing, working or insured in Poland. In contrast, the Tax
register is updated only annually, limiting its temporal resolution.
Furthermore, the PESEL register suffers from well-documented
deregistration issues, where individuals who have left the country may
remain in the database, leading to overestimation of the resident
population.

Although NB-MLE with offset has been rejected as the primary estimator for this application, their results are included here to illustrate that formal model-selection criteria such as AIC and BIC do not handle random-effects models in the same way as fixed-effects models. In particular, the ability to accommodate over-dispersion may unduly favor random-effects models. A related problem is the much narrower range of AIC (or BIC) values as the specification of $\alpha_{it}$ varies, which shows that less information about the mean structure is captured by these criteria. 

When it comes to Poisson PMLE with offset, which is our focus here, there is a~large drop of IC from yearly $\alpha$ (as in exploratory estimation above) to our preferred S8 specification. On the one hand, the interaction between year and Ukraine (separated from the other countries of origin) is the most important reason, as can be seen by comparing S8 to S4 specification. On the other hand, as described in Section \ref{sec-data}, sex is a~factor by which all the counts vary; the small change of IC from S4 to S8 is simply due to the overwhelming mass of males in these data. 

The year-by-Ukrainian interaction in the community anchor is motivated by the 2022 EU Temporary Protection Directive, which expanded the Regularized 
Ukrainian population $N$ without a~similar effect on the unauthorized counts $m$. Without this interaction, the community anchor would have to absorb this structural break as a~declining trend that affects the non-Ukrainians as well.

In addition to the S8 specification of $\alpha_{it}$, with yearly $\beta_t$ and constant $\gamma$, we have also considered other specifications of $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Regarding the exposure elasticity $\beta_{it}$, adding sex or Ukrainian origin to yearly $\beta_t$ does not improve IC and introduces instability, whereas holding $\beta$ constant across years would produce nearly identical population size trajectories by Poisson PMLE and NB-MLE, and misses the structural break noted above. Regarding the offset $\gamma$, allowing it to vary by covariates or time has a~larger effect on Poisson PMLE than NB-MLE, in the sense that IC differs more for the former than the latter, which is similar to the contrast between them in Table \ref{tbl-aic-specs}, although the resulting population size estimates still fall within the confidence intervals by the S8 specification. In short, the S8 specification here balances responsiveness to structural break with estimation stability and model parsimony.

In comparison, the NB-MLE is unstable and can give implausibly high estimates from time to time, such as in 2021 (the year after Covid outbreak) and 2024. Notice that the reference population size has a~steady increase from 2019 to 2022, after which it has remained stable till 2024. Meanwhile, the total of detected unauthorized persons went first down from 6,600 in 2019 to about 3,000 in 2021--2022, before it climbed back to about the same level in 2024 as in 2019. The surge of NB-MLE in 2021 and 2024 has thus no obvious justification in these data.

Nevertheless, the ability of NB-MLE to accommodate over-dispersion in the data makes it possible to use these estimates for sensitivity analysis. In other words, had excessive over-dispersion been present and caused bias to the Poisson PMLE, how might the estimate have looked like by a~method that accommodates over-dispersion? Our reported estimates are robust in this respect, since --- apart from the implausible 2021 and 2024 spikes discussed above --- the NB-MLE point estimates lie within the 95\% confidence intervals by Poisson PMLE, whether or not one finds this necessary given the stable and plausible trajectory of Poisson PMLE.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec-conclusions}

This paper proposes a~general community-anchored modeling framework for estimating the size of unauthorized populations using only aggregated
administrative data. The method requires three inputs: counts of detected unauthorized migrants, the size of an authorized foreign population (as a~reference), and a~covariate proxying detection intensity. 

The framework is applied to the Polish data from 2019 to 2024. Our primary specification allows the community anchor ($\alpha$) to vary by year, Ukrainian origin (with interaction), and sex, while the exposure elasticity ($\beta$) varies by year only, and the offset ($\gamma$) of the detection intensity is constant throughout. In particular, our reported estimate of the adult non-Schengen unauthorized population size is 101,000 (80\% interval: 46,000--177,000; 95\% CI: 29,000--254,000) in 2024, which stands at 8.9\% compared with the ZUS reference population. Males constitute the majority of the unauthorized population. The reported estimates are robust against potential model mis-specification, not least when one takes into account their associated confidence intervals. 


The 2022 EU Temporary Protection Directive massively expanded Ukraine's reference population without a~proportional increase in unauthorized Ukrainians. Our model captures this structural break naturally in terms of the year-by-Ukrainian interaction in the mean specification. This results in plausible as well as stable trajectory of the target population size over the entire period from 2019 to 2024. The framework is applicable to other large-scale regularization or protection programs that selectively inflates the lawful reference populations for specific communities. 

Several limitations should be noted for practitioners. First, the wide confidence intervals reflect the inherent uncertainty in estimating hidden or hard-to-reach populations. Second, the method assumes a~structural relationship between authorized and unauthorized populations that holds across a~number of communities, in order for the model to be identifiable. Abrupt policy changes (such as the Temporary Protection Directive) can violate this assumption, which would require careful effort to revise any existing model. Third, the approach estimates the \emph{stock} of unauthorized individuals at a~given time, not \emph{gross flows}; year-to-year changes in the stock reflect the net balance of entries, exits, regularizations, and removals. Fourth, the
restriction to non-Schengen nationals excludes EU citizens who may overstay or work without authorization.

%The progression from separate yearly NB-MLE, which may produce volatile estimates sensitive to ad hoc regrouping (Section~\ref{sec-assumptions}), to Poisson PMLE with covariate-varying $\alpha$, baseline offset $\gamma$, and clustered bootstrap provides a~general template for applying the community-anchored models to multi-year administrative data. 

The framework transfers easily to other countries, just as it has been from Norway in 2006 to Poland in 2024: it requires only aggregated counts that already exist in administrative systems and can be implemented without individual-level data linkage or new data collection. The accompanying R package \texttt{uncounted} provides a~complete implementation, including estimation, inference, diagnostics, and sensitivity analysis tools.
